What Does Commercial Epoxy Flooring Involve?

Commercial floors get heavier prep and thicker builds than a home garage. We grind with dust-controlled equipment, repair joints and spalled areas, then install a system chosen for your traffic: a high-build epoxy, a broadcast system with aggregate for grip, or a urethane cement for kitchens.

Safety lanes, walkways and parking lines can be striped right into the coating. Large areas are done in phases so part of the building stays open while the rest cures.

When Do You Need Commercial Epoxy Flooring?

Businesses usually call us at one of these points:

  • Bare concrete is dusting onto inventory and equipment
  • A health inspection flagged a floor that's hard to keep clean
  • Forklift traffic has worn through an old coating
  • You're opening a new location or remodeling
  • Fading safety lines need to be redone

Why Do Commercial Floors Break Down?

Commercial concrete takes abuse from steel wheels, dropped tools, oils, cleaning chemicals and, in kitchens, hot water and grease every day. Bare concrete is porous, so it soaks up spills and slowly crumbles at the surface.

Older coatings fail when they were too thin for the traffic or went down over poorly prepared concrete. Matching the system to the use, and grinding properly first, is what makes a commercial floor last.

What Affects the Cost of Commercial Epoxy Flooring in Irving?

Square footage, the type of system and the condition of the slab drive most of the price. Heavy joint repair, old coatings that need removal and moisture issues all add prep time.

Scheduling matters too. Night, weekend or phased installs that keep you open take more planning. We'll walk the site, discuss your traffic and timeline, and give you a written proposal.

Epoxy vs. Urethane Cement: Which Do You Need?

For warehouses, auto shops and showrooms, a high-build epoxy system is usually the right choice. Restaurant kitchens and food prep areas are better served by urethane cement, which stands up to hot water, grease and the smooth, easy-to-clean floor that health inspectors expect.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you install a commercial floor without closing my business?

In most cases, yes. We can work nights and weekends or split the floor into phases so part of the space stays open. Fast-cure systems help shorten each phase.

What floor is best for a restaurant kitchen?

Urethane cement is the usual choice. It handles hot water, grease and temperature swings, and it can be coved up the wall so floors are smooth and easy to clean, which inspectors look for.

Can you add safety lines to a warehouse floor?

Yes. Forklift lanes, walkways, hazard zones and parking stalls can be striped into the coating, so they don't wear off like painted lines.

How long does a commercial epoxy floor last?

It depends on traffic, but a properly built system on a well-prepared slab commonly lasts many years. The topcoat can be renewed as it wears, which keeps the base system protected.
